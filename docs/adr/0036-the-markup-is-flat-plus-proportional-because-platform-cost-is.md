# The markup is flat plus proportional, because Platform Cost is

The markup is sized to recover **Platform Cost** — Stripe's fees plus the supplier platform's own fees — and Platform Cost is not a percentage. Duffel bills $3.00 on every paid order *plus* 1% of order value; Stripe bills a flat fee *plus* a rate. So the recovery mirrors that shape: a flat component and a proportional one, rather than the single percentage the code has used since it was written.

`pricing.ts` had modelled Stripe alone and stated outright that "no profit margin is intended". It did not know Duffel charged anything. The August 2026 invoice (INV07982, $79.12) showed it does: $21.00 of Paid Order at $3.00 × 7, and $58.12 of Managed Content at 1% of $5,812.44 of order value. Adding that 1% + $3.00 to Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 puts break-even at `(0.039·P + 3.30) / (0.971·P)`, which converges to **4.017% as the fare goes to infinity** — above the 4% then charged. There was no ticket price at which the old rate was solvent.

## Excess Search is not the problem, and search volume is not the driver

The invoice's Excess Search line is $0.005 per search and read **zero** — the allowance was not touched. Managed Content, the largest line, is 1% of *order value*, so it scales with what is sold rather than with who browses. This matters because it kills the design that would otherwise have been forced: no booking-volume forecast is needed, no denominator has to be guessed, and no cross-subsidy from bookers to browsers is required. A percentage recovers a percentage automatically at any volume.

## August is not a calibration baseline

Six of the seven orders on that invoice were the team's own live test bookings, later cancelled. Sizing rates against it would have set a ~30% markup to recover a cost that does not recur, and charged every real customer for it in perpetuity. **The invoice is a testing artefact, not a demand signal.** OTV issued a written warning over the same behaviour on their platform in September 2026 — "we kindly ask that you refrain from further test bookings on the production environment" — their second such request. Neither supplier's live environment is a test environment; a live order is placed only when someone intends to honour it. Hotel pricing needs no live booking to verify in any case, because [ADR-0021](0021-the-stripe-base-comes-from-the-prebook-quote.md) takes the Stripe base from the prebook **Quote**, which is a valuation and not a reservation.

## The markup stays refundable, so cancellations are socialised

A cancelled booking costs exactly what a completed one costs: Duffel charged all seven August orders including the six cancelled, and Stripe keeps its fee on a refund. But the refund returns the markup in full ([cancel-booking](../../src/app/api/flights/cancel-booking/route.ts) refunds `piAmount`, markup included), so a cancellation recovers nothing against a cost incurred in full.

Making the markup non-refundable would have recovered it exactly, and was rejected: it is a customer-facing change, it needs consumer-law review in PH and KR, and it would have to sit outside the penalty ratio that [ADR-0023](0023-a-cancellation-refunds-what-the-recorded-terms-allow.md) makes the whole shape of a refund. Instead the cost is spread across everyone, which requires assuming a cancellation rate `c` and solving `M = (3.30 + 0.039·P) / (0.971 − c)`.

That denominator has a **pole at c = 0.971**: past a 97.1% cancellation rate no finite markup recovers anything, because Stripe's cut on the refunded charges exceeds everything collectable. The curve is gentle at low `c` and violent at high `c`, and the choice to socialise is only safe while `c` stays low. It is therefore conditional, and the conditions are written down:

- `c` is measured monthly from real orders, **excluding staff bookings** — otherwise August-style contamination re-enters through the measurement.
- Rates are trued up quarterly. Two consecutive quarters below 12% and the rate comes **down**; that is what keeps "cost recovery, not margin" true rather than aspirational.
- Above ~35%, socialising stops being defensible and the non-refundable fee is reconsidered.

## The fee is invisible until checkout, and that is a known cost

The markup is applied only in `flights/book`, `mobile/flights/book` and `booking/create-payment` — all payment-time. Search shows the raw supplier price. Keeping it that way was chosen deliberately, and it conflicts with the **Display Currency** principle that the figure on screen is the one that will be charged; the glossary has been amended to say the guarantee covers conversion and not the total.

The cost is a checkout jump that grows with the markup, and it is worst where the flat component is largest relative to the fare — 13.6% on a $50 ticket against 5.6% on an $830 one. Because [ADR-0009](0009-airline-order-placed-before-payment.md) places the Duffel order *before* payment, a traveller who balks at that jump has already cost $3.00 + 1%. **Raising the markup therefore raises abandonment, which raises `c`, which raises the markup required** — a loop that runs hardest on the cheapest fares.

Capping the effective fee at 10% of fare bounds the worst case. Two thresholds fall out of that and they are easily confused: the cap **binds** below about $88, where 5.1% + $4.30 first exceeds 10% of the fare, but it only stops **covering Platform Cost** below about $57, where 10% of the fare drops under break-even. Between the two the booking still pays its own way with a thinner cancellation buffer. Below $57 the shortfall grows towards the $3.40 flat break-even as the fare tends to zero and never exceeds it, so the cap's cost is bounded whatever is sold.

## Hotels carry no platform fee yet, and the 5% is a provision

There is no hotel equivalent of the Duffel invoice. The OTV monthly invoice **is the room cost**, drawn on a credit line — `OTV invoice total == SUM(supplier_cost)` — and is already funded by the fare. TravelgateX does charge a connection fee, but the account sits on the **Free** development tier, which [the integration audit](../travelgatex-duffel-audit.md) records as something that "must change before go-live".

So hotels at 5% currently over-recover by roughly 1.2 points against a Stripe-only break-even of about 3.8% at `c = 20%`. That surplus is held as an **earmarked provision against the incoming STD/SUP fee**, not counted as revenue, and the rate is set once when TravelgateX quotes the schedule. Repricing down and then up within a quarter would cost more trust than the surplus is worth.

## Considered Options

- **Raise the single percentage instead (rejected).** Covering a $50 fare needs ~11%, which on an $830 booking collects $90 to recover $37 — a $53 margin, and the best customers subsidising the cheapest. The opposite of what the brand claims.
- **Percentage sized at the average, ~4.5% (rejected).** Solvent on expensive fares, quietly underwater on cheap ones — and cheap fares are the Korean and SEA inventory the 4% rate was raised to serve in the first place.
- **Recover hosting and monitoring too (rejected).** Infrastructure scales with the product, not with bookings, so folding it in makes the fare depend on decisions no traveller can see, and under-recovery worsens structurally over time. **Platform Cost** is deliberately a closed set.
- **All-in pricing in search (rejected, reluctantly).** Removes the checkout jump and the abandonment loop outright, and matches how the OTAs the **Price Promise** is measured against already quote. Rejected in favour of keeping the search-results advantage. The loop above is the accepted cost.
- **Per-brand rates (rejected).** CheapestGo and AirangGo book through one Duffel account and one OTV account, so there is one Platform Cost pool. Where they differ is currency mix, which the per-currency Stripe rate already captures.

## Consequences

- **Stripe's real rate is 4.4%, not 2.9%, and measuring it moved every number in this ADR.** Every live charge on the account settles at 2.9% + 1.5% + $0.30 — the base rate plus the international-card surcharge, because the Stripe account is US-registered and the customers are not. Six live charges on 2026-09-08, all Philippine cards, every one landing on 4.40% to the cent. Six is enough because the fee is a deterministic tier rather than a distribution; a larger sample would establish the *mix* across markets, not precision within one. The 1.5 points cost more than the entire Duffel gap that prompted this ADR, and — unlike the flat component — the error was proportional, so it cost most on the largest bookings: $40.92 on a $2,000 booking against $2.11 on a $100 one.
- **The revised rates, and why each moved:** flights $4.30 + 5.1% → **$4.40 + 7.2%**; hotels 5% → **$0.40 + 5.9%**; cap 10% → **12%**; `HOTEL_FX_DISPLAY_TOLERANCE` 0.5% → **0.3%**.
  - The **cap** moved because what was agreed at 10% was a *fare threshold*, not a percentage: it bound below $88. At the measured rate a 10% cap would have bound below $153, capping well inside loss-making territory. 12% restores the threshold to ~$90, with bare cost uncovered only below ~$54.
  - The **hotel provision never existed.** Hotels were held at 5% on the belief that it over-recovered by ~1.2 points and so carried a reserve against TravelgateX's incoming connection fee. At 4.4% a $300 stay netted $0.84: 5% covered a hotel only if nobody ever cancelled. There is still no provision — when TravelgateX quotes STD/SUP the rate rises again by whatever it is.
  - The **FX tolerance** had become larger than the margin it was meant to sit inside. At the old rate a $300 hotel left 1.77% of the charge and 0.5% consumed 28% of it; at 4.4% the buffer fell to 0.27%, so a gap *inside* tolerance — silently absorbed by design — made the booking a loss. Raising the hotel rate restores the buffer to ~1.19%, and 0.3% keeps the same 28% share the original design intended.
- **A KRW charge will cost more again.** It is international *and* converted, so roughly 5.4%. AirangGo is Korea-locked, so its launch changes the rate. It is deliberately not pre-set to 5.4%, because that would over-recover a full point on every Philippine booking — margin under another name. **Re-run `scratch/stripe-fees.mjs` and read the KR tier before AirangGo takes live bookings**, rather than learning it from a month of under-recovery; failing that, the monthly reconciliation notifies on drift beyond half a point.
- **The estimate is now checked rather than assumed.** `balance_transaction` was read nowhere in the codebase, so nothing could ever have told us the rate was wrong — the same blindness that let a 4% markup sit below a 4.017% floor for months. Both booking paths now record what Stripe actually took: flights into `booking_financial_events.metadata`, hotels into `bookings.provider_metadata`. Neither needed a migration, and neither can fail a booking — the fee is best-effort by construction. The ledger's `supplier_reconciliation` event type, declared in the original schema and never once written, is the intended home for the monthly Duffel side.
- **The sandbox says Stripe's fee is already not one rate.** A 90-day sample of 88 test charges (2026-09-08) split into two exact populations rather than scattering around an average: 2.900% on 54% of gross and 3.900% on 46%, each holding to ±0.01 point. One percentage point apart is Stripe's currency-conversion surcharge, and the 3.46% blended figure concealed it entirely — which is why `scratch/stripe-fees.mjs` now reports tiers rather than a mean. **This is test-mode data and every charge in it was USD**, so it does not establish what a Korean or Philippine card costs and no rate was changed on the strength of it. What it does establish is that a single `STRIPE_RATE` constant is a simplification of a schedule with steps in it, and that 2.9% is the bottom step rather than the usual one.
- **`toStripeAmount` had no inverse, and `pi.amount / 100` was hardcoded in fourteen places.** KRW is zero-decimal, so every one of them understated a won figure by 100× — including the refund quote shown to a traveller before they cancel, which offered ₩12,000 back on a ₩1,200,000 booking. The charge and the refund themselves were always correct, because both stay in minor units; it was every *derived and displayed* figure that was wrong. `fromStripeAmount` now exists and all fourteen use it. The bug was latent only because no KRW booking had been taken yet — and AirangGo is Korea-locked.
- **The reverse-derivation in `enrichBookingFinances` becomes wrong.** It recovers supplier cost as `totalAmount / (1 + markupRate)`, which has no term for a flat fee, and it is the fallback for every booking with missing financial data — so it would silently corrupt admin revenue reporting rather than fail.
- **The bundle discount had no cost saving behind it.** Bundling swaps `HOTEL_MARKUP` for `BUNDLE_MARKUP` and nothing else; a bundle is still one Duffel order, one OTV booking and two Stripe charges. The only real saving available is the $0.30 of a merged PaymentIntent. The advertised ~0.9% was funded from the hotel provision, which is spoken for.
- **The flat component is USD-denominated** and must be converted at the **Locked Rate** per [ADR-0008](0008-fx-locked-at-booking-in-usd.md), not hardcoded per currency. KRW is zero-decimal, and `round2` on a flat fee does not account for that.
- **Paid Ancillary is $2.00 per item and recovers nothing today.** It read zero in August only because no seats or bags were sold. Selling ancillaries without pricing this in reintroduces the same hole this ADR closes.
- **Excess Search is $0.005 beyond an allowance whose size is not recorded anywhere.** It is zero now; growth will cross it, and nothing currently watches for that.
