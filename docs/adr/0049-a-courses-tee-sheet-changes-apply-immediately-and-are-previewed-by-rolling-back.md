# A course's tee-sheet changes apply immediately, and are previewed by rolling back

Course staff change their own tee sheet from the portal: schedules, prices, blocks and extras.
These changes go live at once, are recorded in a history, and are shown to the course with their
impact before they are applied. This records why each of those is the way it is.

**Immediately, not queued for approval.** The course is the authority on its own inventory. A
review step would put the team back in the middle of every price edit, which is the round trip the
portal exists to remove. What makes this safe is not approval but scope and protection: a person
can only change courses they are attached to (ADR-0048); a booking keeps the price it locked when
checkout started, so a price change can only affect tee times nobody has booked; and the team can
see every change and override it. A course cannot publish, unpublish, delete or re-zone itself.

**A preview is the real change, rolled back.** Each mutating function takes `dryRun` and runs in a
transaction that is rolled back after returning its counts. The alternative, a separate "what would
this do" calculation, is a second implementation of the same rules, and the day it drifts the
course confirms one thing and gets another. Running the change itself cannot disagree with itself.
The cost is that a preview takes the same locks and time as the change; for one course's tee sheet
that is milliseconds.

**A block stops new sales; it never touches a booking.** Closing a tee time that already has a
booking could mean cancelling, refunding or moving that booking, each of which belongs to the team
(or, later, a refund flow). So a block only sets the tee time to closed, which means nothing new is
sold on it, and returns the references of the live bookings sitting on it so the course can sort
them out. Likewise editing a schedule leaves a tee time with a live booking at its price and spots.
`closed_by_block_id` records which block closed a tee time, so removing a block reopens exactly
those, and a tee time two blocks cover stays closed until both are gone. A tee time closed because
its schedule was deleted has no block and never reopens.

**Blocks are data, applied at generation.** A block is a row, not a one-off deletion, so tee times
generated tomorrow honour it. Deleting a day's tee times instead would lose the booking history
and silently come back the next time generation ran.

## Considered options

- **Queue every change for admin approval.** Rejected, as above.
- **Guardrails** (hold a price change beyond ±N% for approval). Deferred: cheap to add on top of
  this if a course ever does something unwise; the history already makes it visible.
- **Separate preview calculation.** Rejected for the drift reason above.
- **Delete tee times to close a day.** Rejected: unrecoverable, and regenerated by the next run.
